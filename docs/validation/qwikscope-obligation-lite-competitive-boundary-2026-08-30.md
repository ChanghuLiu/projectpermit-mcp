# QwikScope obligation-lite competitive boundary — 2026-08-30

## Purpose

Evaluate QwikScope as a direct form competitor to ProjectPermit's proposed licence-light municipal process-obligation fallback.

The question is not whether QwikScope has a website. It is whether the market already contains a materially similar product shape:

> project/site facts -> permit set -> fees/timeline/risk -> required submittal documents -> API/workflow delivery

If yes, ProjectPermit must not treat `permit-process obligation bundle` as empty whitespace merely because the data can be assembled from public municipal sources.

## 1. QwikScope current product surface

QwikScope LLC publicly describes a U.S.-wide property/deployment intelligence platform with six live specialized modules and coverage across 3,000+ U.S. counties.

Main site:

- https://qwikscope.com/

Public claims include:

- 26 source authorities;
- 50+ data layers;
- 3,000+ U.S. counties;
- six specialized modules live;
- self-serve and engagement/API delivery paths;
- source attribution / cited claims throughout the product family.

Its low-friction paid entry is Maps at `$9.99/month`; Maps + Qwikly is publicly shown at `$19.99/month`. The public site does not expose a comparable fixed public price for Greenlight or Outline in the research pass on 2026-08-30.

Sources:

- https://qwikscope.com/
- https://qwikscope.com/maps/

## 2. Greenlight — very close to ProjectPermit permit/process scope

Greenlight takes a project and scope, resolves the controlling jurisdiction and returns a permitting plan.

Current public output includes:

- required / likely / conditional permits;
- issuing agency for each permit;
- jurisdiction-specific triggers;
- building, grading, floodplain, zoning, telecom and utility permits;
- dependency-ordered permit sequence;
- critical path;
- min/max weeks per permit;
- blocking vs parallel steps;
- total time-to-approval range;
- fee estimate per permit;
- approval/schedule risk flags;
- zoning/flood/historic/hearing triggers;
- interactive dashboard;
- PDF strategy brief;
- API access `on engagement`.

Source:

- https://qwikscope.com/greenlight/

### Competitive overlap

This overlaps strongly with ProjectPermit's current/fallback concepts around:

- permit determination;
- official/jurisdiction-specific evidence;
- fees;
- timeline;
- workflow sequencing;
- risk/conditional states;
- API delivery.

Therefore `permit + fee + timeline + risk` is **not an uncontested product category**.

## 3. Outline — direct overlap with municipal obligation-lite

Outline starts from the permit determination and derives the project-specific submittal set.

Current public output includes:

- studies, reports and exhibits;
- application forms per permit;
- supporting documents;
- narratives/justifications;
- agency-specific attachments;
- civil/site/architectural/construction drawing sets;
- discipline grouping;
- required vs conditional classification;
- each item traced to the permit requirement that drives it;
- dashboard / checklist / PDF;
- Greenlight handoff;
- API access `on engagement`.

Source:

- https://qwikscope.com/outline/

### Competitive overlap

This is extremely close to the proposed ProjectPermit `submission_documents` / `professional_or_designer_requirements` / `related_approvals` obligation layer.

It removes any basis for claiming that a structured `what must I submit?` bundle is itself a unique moat.

## 4. Delivery and commercial model

QwikScope publicly supports several modes across its product family:

- self-serve dashboard;
- API access;
- embeddable widget;
- full platform engagement / custom integrations / white-labeling;
- portfolio/batch engagements for some modules.

Its public privacy policy also states that billing uses Stripe and that the service combines third-party/public/government data with customer queries to produce analyses.

Sources:

- https://qwikscope.com/lookout/
- https://qwikscope.com/trace/
- https://qwikscope.com/privacy/

For Greenlight/Outline specifically, the public pages say API access is available `on engagement`; a public developer portal, open API specification, MCP endpoint or x402/pay-per-call machine marketplace route was **not found** in this research pass.

That distinction may matter for ProjectPermit's machine-native distribution thesis, but it is a delivery-channel distinction, not a unique core dataset/result distinction.

## 5. Current maturity / traction boundary

QwikScope appears to have a real, broad current product surface rather than a single marketing placeholder:

- six modules are labelled live;
- Greenlight and Outline state that users can sign in and run them;
- Maps has explicit self-serve subscription pricing;
- Lookout/Trace describe concrete API/engagement modes;
- Trace describes a national-portfolio AT&T audit as the basis of the module.

However, broad web searches excluding QwikScope's own domain on 2026-08-30 did **not** surface meaningful independent evidence such as:

- recognized funding announcements;
- independent customer case studies;
- substantial third-party reviews;
- press coverage;
- external adoption metrics for Greenlight/Outline;
- public transaction/call volume;
- public Greenlight/Outline contract pricing.

The current website's privacy policy identifies the provider as QwikScope LLC in Vine Grove, Kentucky, but the research pass did not establish company age or scale from a strong independent source.

### Classification

The most defensible present classification is:

> **strong form competitor; apparently live/early-commercial product; independent traction unverified.**

Do not call QwikScope a mature incumbent or market leader without further evidence.

## 6. Geography / ICP distinction

QwikScope is strongly U.S.-oriented and its broader product family emphasizes property/site feasibility, telecom infrastructure, tower ownership/leases, AEC and deployment intelligence.

ProjectPermit is currently focused on:

- Canadian municipal permit rules;
- contractor / field-service / preconstruction workflows;
- Jobber/ServiceM8-like work records;
- deterministic action bundles;
- safe writeback proposals;
- MCP/x402 / agent-native discovery.

This leaves a plausible **geography + workflow + delivery** wedge even though the output shape overlaps.

But that wedge is not enough by itself. Canada is smaller, and Canadian technical code licensing can be restrictive. A Canada-only clone of Greenlight/Outline could have worse economics than QwikScope's U.S. model.

## 7. Differentiation implication

ProjectPermit must not proceed with municipal obligation-lite under the thesis:

> `nobody else turns permit rules into documents/fees/timelines.`

That thesis is false based on QwikScope's current product description.

A defensible ProjectPermit differentiation would need to come from a combination such as:

1. **Canada-specific maintained municipal/provincial coverage** where U.S. platforms do not operate;
2. **contractor quote/job insertion**, not general property due diligence;
3. **deterministic workflow consequence** — task/quote/schedule/writeback actions, not just report output;
4. **source freshness/change identity** tied to repeat work records;
5. **machine-native low-friction delivery** through OpenAPI + MCP + x402/free preview;
6. **licensed technical-code layer later**, if buyer demand and rights support it;
7. **platform licence/minimum commitment** for high-frequency integrated buyers rather than relying only on per-call pricing.

Even these remain hypotheses until a buyer values them.

## 8. Impact on municipal obligation-lite build gate

QwikScope raises the build threshold.

The fallback should now be built only if a real Canadian buyer/platform establishes both:

### Demand

- bounded monthly project/quote volume;
- one or more municipal process obligations repeatedly matter;
- those obligations materially change quote scope/cost/schedule/handoff.

### Differentiation

- the buyer cannot already obtain an equivalent result cheaply from its current workflow/vendor;
- Canadian municipality-specific coverage matters;
- machine/work-record integration matters enough to prefer ProjectPermit over a dashboard/report product;
- maintaining the source layer externally is preferred over internal maintenance.

Without those answers, building obligation-lite risks becoming a low-moat Canadian clone of an already demonstrated product form.

## 9. Evidence / score impact

No E-level increase.

QwikScope is competitor evidence, not ProjectPermit buyer evidence.

It has two opposite effects:

### Positive

- independently validates the product form;
- confirms that permit determination can naturally expand into fees/timelines/risk/submittal requirements;
- validates API/engagement as a delivery model.

### Negative

- removes novelty from the obligation-lite output shape;
- raises differentiation requirements;
- increases the risk that accessible municipal process data is reproducible rather than a strong moat.

## Bottom line

**Municipal obligation-lite is viable as a product form but is not whitespace.**

QwikScope already publicly describes a very similar U.S. chain:

`project/site -> permits -> fees/timeline/risk -> required submittal documents -> API/report/workflow`

Its independent traction is currently unverified, so this is not evidence that the category is saturated.

For ProjectPermit, the correct response is not to abandon the fallback and not to build it speculatively. The build gate becomes stricter:

> **Only proceed when a Canadian buyer proves a repeated material workflow and specifically values Canada-specific maintained requirements + machine/work-record integration enough to differentiate from report/dashboard competitors.**
