# Canada design-partner specimen matrix — 2026-08-30

## Purpose

Ground the Layer-C buyer archetype in real Canadian contractor businesses that already use Jobber or Buildxact and whose work overlaps ProjectPermit's current project families.

This note is **public-market research only**. It is not buyer evidence, does not authorize outreach, and does not change E2/E3/E4/E5.

The goal is to avoid validating against an abstract `contractor` persona. The next buyer conversation should use the same recent-work denominator across very different contractor sizes:

> **Among your last 20 estimates, how many required permit / code / engineering / document / inspection research before the quote could be finalized?**

Then ask what changed and how the work is handled today.

### ProjectPermit family fit

Very high current-family overlap:
- interior renovation;
- basement;
- kitchen/bath plumbing;
- dwelling/space changes may occur in full-house projects;
- additions / structural scope can occur in larger renovations.

### Why this is a strong archetype

This specimen combines four attributes that the current validation model says matter:

1. **multi-municipality exposure** — jurisdiction variation is not hypothetical;
2. **estimate-before-work workflow** — Jobber is used in the business category and the company solicits project quote requests;
3. **permit/code responsibility is already part of the delivered service**;
4. **team scale** is large enough that maintained knowledge may have an internal coordination cost rather than living only in one owner's head.

### What public data does NOT establish

Public material does not tell us:
- estimates per month;
- permit/code-sensitive estimates per month;
- minutes/hours spent researching each estimate;
- whether knowledge is centralized in one employee or distributed across staff;
- whether Summit would buy a maintained external capability rather than continue internal maintenance;
- willingness to pay.

Therefore this remains an **archetype specimen, not E2**.

## Standard `last 20 estimates` E2 gate

Use exactly the same denominator across these archetypes so results are comparable.

### Q1 — bounded denominator

> Looking only at your **last 20 estimates / quote-stage projects**, how many required you or your team to check current permit, building-code, engineer/designer, required-document, inspection, heritage/zoning or similar local requirements before you could finalize scope or price?

Record integer `0..20`.

### Q2 — material consequence

For those affected estimates, count how many changed at least one of:
- quote price / allowance;
- quoted scope;
- engineer / architect / designer involvement;
- required drawings / documents;
- schedule / start date;
- permit / approval path;
- inspection / sequencing milestone;
- decision not to quote / proceed.

### Q3 — current workflow

For a typical affected estimate, who does the research now?
- owner;
- estimator;
- project coordinator / office admin;
- designer / engineer;
- permit consultant / expeditor;
- city call / portal lookup;
- combination.

### Q4 — time per affected estimate

Use bounded ranges:
- `<10 min`;
- `10–30 min`;
- `30–60 min`;
- `1–2 h`;
- `2 h+`.

### Q5 — externalization

Ask the build-vs-buy boundary directly:

> If a maintained external service returned the current requirement bundle with official evidence and clearly marked unknowns, would you prefer to use that service, or keep maintaining / researching the rules internally?

Allowed outcomes:
- definitely external;
- likely external if reliable/current;
- depends on price / jurisdiction coverage;
- prefer internal;
- existing consultant/professional already solves it.

### Q6 — natural payment unit

Do not lead with x402. Ask which commercial shape is natural:
- fixed monthly add-on;
- per-project / per-estimate;
- annual licence;
- included in existing software subscription;
- would not pay separately.

## Proposed interpretation bands

These bands are validation rules, not forecasts.

### Weak frequency

`0–2 / last 20 estimates`

Even if useful, likely too sparse for a standalone recurring contractor product unless consequence/value is unusually high.

### Moderate frequency

`3–7 / last 20`

Potentially viable when research time is meaningful and results change quote/scope/schedule. Strong candidate for an embedded add-on or modest fixed monthly price.

### Strong frequency

`8+ / last 20`

High-frequency regulatory-sensitive workflow. If the buyer also prefers external maintenance and reports material consequence, this is the strongest path toward E2/E3.

### Strongest evidence package

Do not promote Layer C because of frequency alone.

A strong buyer package requires:

`recent denominator + material consequence + repeated research time + preference to externalize + plausible payment unit`

## Current-jurisdiction fit gate

The specimen research exposed an important deployment constraint: a good buyer archetype is not automatically a good **current pilot**.

Current ProjectPermit municipalities are:
- Toronto;
- Ottawa;
- Mississauga;
- Vancouver;
- Laval;
- Gatineau;
- Longueuil.

The public specimens above are not uniformly inside that footprint:

- Summit is Richmond Hill / York Region based but explicitly serves Toronto and therefore has at least some current-coverage overlap;
- Guest Plumbing explicitly operates in Toronto and therefore also has current-coverage overlap;
- Akins Carpentry is based in Stirling-Rawdon, currently unsupported;
- Renovation Revolution is Kingston-based, currently unsupported.

### Pilot rule

Do **not** add a municipality merely to make an attractive archetype fit the product.

A buyer can enter a current E3 pilot only through one of two paths:

1. the buyer can provide representative recent work records from an already-supported municipality; or
2. the buyer first produces strong E2 evidence (`recent denominator + material consequence + externalization`) for an unsupported municipality, making one tightly scoped new jurisdiction a buyer-driven expansion rather than speculative city accumulation.

### Consequence for the current specimens

- Summit-like buyer: potentially eligible **only on Toronto work records** without expansion;
- Guest Plumbing: potentially eligible **only on Toronto renovation/plumbing-relevant records**;
- Akins / Renovation Revolution: archetype/falsification evidence only until a buyer-driven expansion gate is crossed.

This prevents distribution research from quietly reopening the old `add more cities` loop.

## Build-vs-buy warning from Buildxact preconstruction research

Buildxact/Farnsworth public research reports that **53% of builders/remodelers say they are open to doing their own preconstruction tasks such as takeoffs and estimates**.

Source:
- https://www.buildxact.com/ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2023/06/How-Dealers-Use-Saas-to-Modernize-the_Pro-Builder-Relationship_US_2022.pdf

This is not specific to regulatory research, but it is a useful warning against assuming that high workflow volume implies a desire to outsource.

Layer C must prove a different burden:

> not `I don't want to estimate`, but `I don't want to continuously maintain current multi-jurisdiction regulatory knowledge and evidence inside my estimating workflow`.

## Decision consequence

The public specimen research changes **who to validate**, not the evidence score.

Priority order for buyer validation now:

1. **Summit-like multi-municipality residential remodel/design-build company with supported-city records**;
2. **Akins-like small builder/remodeler already using estimate software** as an archetype, but do not expand geography before E2;
3. **Renovation-Revolution-like solo contractor** as low-frequency falsification boundary;
4. multi-city plumbing/HVAC businesses only through current family/municipality overlap or after a separately validated wedge.

No E2/E3/E4/E5 change.

Do not build marketplace integrations, add municipalities, or buy protected code content until at least one real buyer produces a recent bounded denominator plus material consequence and externalization evidence.
