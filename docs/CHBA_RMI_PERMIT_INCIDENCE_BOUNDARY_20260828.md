# CHBA RMI Permit-Incidence Boundary — 2026-08-28

## Purpose

The CHBA Renovation Market Index is currently one of the strongest independent routes for measuring whether ProjectPermit's upstream problem exists at useful frequency across Canadian renovators.

Unlike vendor/platform outreach, the RMI is a national renovator research panel and already demonstrates that CHBA can ask permit-specific special questions and publish regional results.

The important boundary is that the Spring 2026 public permit question measured **approval delay after an application exists**, not **how often permit applicability is unresolved before a quote/contract**.

## What the public Spring 2026 RMI proves

CHBA describes the RMI as a panel of renovation-focused member businesses surveyed on current conditions, future expectations, client inquiries/project pipelines, plus quarterly special questions on current industry issues.

Source:

- `https://www.chba.ca/rmi/`

The Spring 2026 special questions explicitly asked renovators for the average time a building permit takes **between application and approval**, excluding development permits.

The published regional averages include:

- British Columbia: about **18 weeks**;
- Ontario: about **9 weeks**;
- Alberta: about **9 weeks**;
- Manitoba / New Brunswick: about **7 weeks**;
- Newfoundland and Labrador: about **6 weeks**;
- Nova Scotia: about **5 weeks**;
- Saskatchewan: about **3 weeks**.

Source:

- `https://www.chba.ca/rmi/`

This is strong evidence that permit workflow burden is measurable and material enough for CHBA to include in national renovator research.

It is not evidence for ProjectPermit's specific preflight demand because every respondent answering that question is already conceptually downstream of `a building permit application exists`.

## Panel scale / representativeness route

CHBA's renovator communications state that the inaugural RMI pilot received **over 200 survey submissions from across Canada** and that CHBA wants more participation to strengthen regional breakdowns.

Source:

- `https://hub.chba.ca/ap/EmailViewer/pQQE5Gep`

CHBA also states that its broader membership is roughly 8,500 companies spanning builders, renovators, trade contractors and related residential-construction businesses.

Source:

- `https://www.chba.ca/`

The 8,500 membership figure is not the RMI sample and must not be used as the denominator for permit incidence. The relevant fact is that an established national panel exists and has already generated 200+ responses in its pilot.

## The exact missing measurement

ProjectPermit needs a different denominator from `how long did approval take?`.

The useful survey unit is closer to:

> In a recent bounded period, among residential renovation opportunities/quotes you evaluated, how many required someone to research whether a municipal building permit was required **before the contract was signed**?

A second question can separate true ProjectPermit candidates from permit-positive jobs that were already obvious:

> Of those pre-contract cases, how many were genuinely uncertain at first versus projects where your team already knew a permit would be required from the project type/scope?

Ideally the response can also be segmented by project family and region without collecting customer-identifying information.

## Why this route is stronger than individual replies

A single contractor, permit consultant or software vendor can supply useful workflow evidence, but their reply is vulnerable to selection effects:

- permit consultants disproportionately see permit-positive work;
- specialty contractors may already know their permit path;
- software vendors may not observe the municipal research step;
- one dismissive or enthusiastic reply has no denominator.

A neutral renovator panel can directly measure prevalence across different project types and regions.

That is exactly why CHBA RMI evidence could move ProjectPermit's commercial score while generic association statements about permitting cost/delay should not.

## Existing outreach status

ProjectPermit already sent CHBA RMI a permit-workflow follow-up on 2026-08-27 and a second concise follow-up proposing two short future-survey questions.

No additional duplicate outreach should be sent now. Silence is not negative evidence.

The public RMI evidence should instead be used to sharpen how any eventual reply is interpreted.

## Upgrade / negative conditions

### Material positive evidence

An RMI-style result would upgrade the project if it shows, in a bounded regional sample:

- repeated pre-contract permit-applicability research;
- material share of cases falling into ProjectPermit's supported/common renovation families;
- enough monthly project cadence that aggregation can support useful API call volume;
- ideally a non-trivial fraction where municipality-specific research changes scope, price, timing or go/no-go decisions.

### Material negative evidence

The RMI route would materially weaken ProjectPermit if representative respondents show:

- permit applicability is usually already known before quoting;
- research is rare relative to total renovation opportunities;
- uncertain cases are mostly outside supported/common families;
- the meaningful pain is almost entirely downstream approval delay rather than upstream applicability;
- municipalities/consultants already resolve the pre-contract question with little cost.

## Score implication

**No score change from the public RMI page alone.**

The Spring 2026 findings validate that permitting delay is a real, measurable renovator burden and that CHBA has a capable survey channel. They do not supply the missing upstream applicability denominator.

The correct action is to elevate CHBA RMI as a high-quality representative measurement route, not to treat approval-delay evidence as proof of ProjectPermit demand.

## Bottom line

CHBA RMI is now a much more precisely defined validation route:

> **The panel already measures permit process burden; the missing question is whether a material share of renovation opportunities contain an unresolved `permit required?` decision before contract.**

A representative answer to that question would be materially more decision-useful than several additional unbounded one-off outreach replies.
