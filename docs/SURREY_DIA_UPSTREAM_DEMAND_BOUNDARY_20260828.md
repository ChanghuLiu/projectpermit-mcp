# Surrey DIA Upstream Regulatory-Inquiry Demand Boundary — 2026-08-28

## Why this matters

ProjectPermit's commercial uncertainty is not whether municipalities receive any permit/development questions. The harder question is whether there is enough **repeated upstream applicability demand** to support a paid external machine contract.

The City of Surrey's **Development Inquiry Assistant (DIA)** provides one of the clearest current Canadian operational benchmarks found so far because the City publishes actual usage metrics rather than only feature descriptions.

Surrey is **not** one of ProjectPermit's seven currently supported municipalities, so this evidence must not be counted as covered-geography demand or current-family SAM.

## What DIA is

Surrey's April 2024 Council report says the City established DIA to provide instant answers about **permitting requirements and development opportunities**. The initial pilot focused on:

- single-family building permits;
- tenant-improvement building permits.

The December 2024 official-release report says DIA answers questions about permitting requirements and development opportunities and expanded into subdivisions, CD zones and broader Planning & Development pages.

Current City pages describe it as a tool for questions about:

- renovating;
- building;
- development;
- zoning and development requirements.

## Published operational usage

### December 2024 snapshot

Corporate Report R257, dated December 11, 2024, states that DIA was averaging approximately:

> **460 inquiries per month**

with a **1.9% user dissatisfaction rate**.

This number followed the April 2024 pilot and should be treated as a late-2024 operating snapshot, not a permanent/current run-rate.

### February 2026 snapshot

Surrey's second-year Housing Accelerator Fund update, dated February 10, 2026, states that after the December 17, 2024 official launch DIA had helped answer:

> **3,460+ questions**

with a reported:

> **94% confidence rate**

The same report again describes DIA as supporting clients inquiring about permitting requirements, with a focus on single-family building permits and tenant-improvement permits.

Official source:

- https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2026-R042.pdf

## Do not mix the two usage snapshots

The 2024 report's `460 inquiries/month` was a then-current average around the pilot/official-launch period.

The 2026 report's `3,460+ questions since launch` is a cumulative number using the December 17, 2024 official launch as its stated reference point.

A naive division of 3,460 by the roughly 14 months between official launch and the February 2026 report is on the order of **250 questions/month**, not 460/month. But ProjectPermit should **not** claim that 250/month is the actual 2026 run-rate either, because:

- the reports may use different counting windows/definitions;
- usage can be uneven over time;
- release scope changed materially;
- the 3,460 figure is explicitly a minimum (`over 3460`);
- no monthly time series was published.

The defensible conclusion is only that DIA has demonstrated **thousands of real questions and a hundreds-per-month order of magnitude** in at least part of its operating history.

## Why this is valuable evidence

This is materially stronger than generic statements that permit research is painful.

It demonstrates that a Canadian municipality can observe enough repeated pre-application/development information demand to deploy a dedicated assistant and receive substantial real usage.

It also shows that digital self-service can displace routine front-counter work: Surrey explicitly says DIA significantly reduced routine inquiries handled by frontline staff.

That supports the reality of an upstream regulatory-information workflow.

## Why this is NOT ProjectPermit call volume

DIA's question universe is much broader than ProjectPermit's present contract.

It includes, among other things:

- zoning;
- development opportunities;
- subdivisions;
- CD zones;
- permitting process information;
- tenant improvements;
- general building/renovation questions.

The published reports do not disclose what share of DIA questions are specifically:

`Does this proposed scope require a permit?`

They also do not disclose how many questions map to ProjectPermit's eight normalized families.

Surrey is outside current ProjectPermit coverage.

Therefore:

- do **not** count 460/month as ProjectPermit candidate calls;
- do **not** count 3,460 as E4 usage for ProjectPermit;
- do **not** extrapolate Surrey usage across Canadian municipalities;
- do **not** use this to satisfy the `>=500 current-family candidate events/month in covered geography` rescue gate.

DIA is one digital channel and also cannot be treated as an upper bound on total municipal inquiry demand, because users can still use staff/front-counter/other inquiry channels.

## Score impact

**No score change.**

### Pain intensity

The result strengthens already-high pain/workflow evidence, but pain intensity is already **8/10**. One municipality's broad inquiry assistant does not justify another point without representative evidence that the narrow ProjectPermit decision itself is common and consequential.

### Addressable call volume / repeat frequency

The numbers make hundreds-per-month upstream regulatory questions plausible for a large city, but the relevant subset is unknown and Surrey is not covered. They therefore do not justify raising the current call-volume or repeat-frequency ratings.

They also do not justify lowering the ratings: DIA is only one channel and cannot be interpreted as the total demand ceiling.

## What would make Surrey score-moving

Surrey becomes materially score-moving if a future public report or operator provides a breakdown such as:

- number/share of DIA questions that ask whether a permit is required;
- number/share within renovation/current ProjectPermit families;
- repeat users or professional/contractor usage;
- how many DIA answers change the next application/quote decision;
- whether municipal staff still need manual interpretation for most applicability questions;
- whether external software can call DIA/its knowledge layer programmatically.

A clean category breakdown could provide the first useful public conversion factor from broad municipal regulatory inquiries to ProjectPermit-like candidate calls.

Until then, Surrey DIA is a **current operational demand benchmark**, not a SAM estimate and not a rescue signal.
