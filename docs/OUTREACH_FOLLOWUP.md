## Reply handling

Do not send another canned follow-up after a reply. Read the response and classify it:

- `A` — integration interest
- `B` — workflow confirmed
- `C` — weak pain
- `D` — wrong layer / already solved
- `E` — coverage blocker
- `F` — procurement/security blocker

For A/B replies, ask for the smallest next proof:

- 20 anonymized scopes; or
- an approximate monthly workflow count; or
- one developer testing the free MCP preview.

Do not ask all ten discovery questions at once.

## Risk controls

- Never claim guaranteed permit accuracy or municipal authorization.
- Do not send repeated follow-ups after opt-out or negative response.
- Do not request customer PII or confidential project details.
- Do not send attachments in cold follow-up unless requested.
- Keep x402/crypto/payment out of initial validation unless the recipient asks about commercial transport.
- Do not interpret an out-of-office auto-response as market interest.
- Do not count a polite acknowledgement as a validated workflow.

## Success signal

A follow-up is successful only if it creates evidence such as:

- actual monthly job/work-order/project volume;
- a repeated permit-research step;
- anonymized scopes for testing;
- developer integration interest;
- missing jurisdiction tied to expected call volume;
- pricing feedback tied to expected volume.

Reply count alone is not a success metric.
