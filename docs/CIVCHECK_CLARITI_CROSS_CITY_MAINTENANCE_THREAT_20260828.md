# CivCheck / Clariti Cross-City Regulatory-Maintenance Threat — 2026-08-28

## Why this is different from earlier local-checker evidence

The canonical scorecard already penalizes ProjectPermit because a one-city/few-city permit checker can be cheap enough for a contractor, municipality or vertical SaaS to internalize.

CivCheck is materially different evidence. It is not another local checker. It is a commercial govtech platform being configured across multiple jurisdictions to ingest and maintain municipal/regional permit, zoning and code requirements, then run applicant/staff regulatory checks in real workflows.

This matters specifically to the **remaining defensibility** thesis, which had been resting on externally valued cross-city maintenance, accuracy history, evidence/safety and embedded distribution rather than ownership of individual rules.

## Toronto: current Canadian production pilot

On August 27, 2026, the City of Toronto launched a one-year Building Permit Application Pre-Check pilot hosted on CivCheck.

The City says the tool:

- compares uploaded permit-application documents against a defined set of rules, requirements and bylaws;
- checks completeness plus selected zoning / applicable-law / Ontario Building Code requirements;
- currently covers residential buildings with two units or fewer, additions, alterations, garden/laneway suites, decks, porches and detached garages;
- returns `likely compliant`, `needs attention` or `not compliant` findings;
- produces a pre-check report for later City submission;
- requires City staff to perform the final formal review and decision.

Toronto's workflow begins after the applicant already has a permit type. Therefore this is **not** evidence that CivCheck answers ProjectPermit's upstream `does this scope require a permit?` contract.

## Seattle: independent municipal accuracy benchmark

Seattle's Innovation & Performance team and Seattle Department of Construction and Inspections ran a proof-of-value study with CivCheck from March through October 2025 and published results in June 2026.

The study used real residential permit applications and compared CivCheck's checks with City-staff review.

Seattle reported:

- **87% accuracy** for application-completeness checks;
- **92% accuracy** for design-compliance checks;
- accuracy improved during the test as CivCheck was further calibrated;
- many commonly failed compliance checks could be automated;
- full compliance coverage remains harder because Seattle's codes contain thousands of requirements and some require difficult interpretation;
- the City recommended a production pilot for pre-screening application completeness.

Seattle also interviewed 20 former applicants and more than a dozen staff members.

Independent source:

- https://innovation-hub.seattle.gov/2026/06/17/ai-construction-permitting-seattle-civcheck-study/

This is materially stronger than vendor-only accuracy marketing. It shows that the same commercial platform can be calibrated to a different city's rules and produce useful measured results against staff review.

## Why this is score-moving even though CivCheck is downstream

CivCheck does **not** currently demonstrate the same product contract as ProjectPermit. Its public workflow generally assumes the applicant already knows the permit/application type and has documents to pre-screen.

Therefore this finding does **not** reduce competitive headroom again. Competitive headroom is already 0/10 and CivCheck is not an exact upstream substitute.

It does, however, independently weaken ProjectPermit's remaining **defensibility** rationale.

Before this finding, the remaining 2/10 defensibility could still point to a combination of:

- maintained cross-city regulatory knowledge;
- calibration / accuracy history;
- safe reviewer-oriented regulatory automation;
- integration into real permit workflows;
- distribution to municipalities/software platforms.

CivCheck now demonstrates that a commercial third party can productize several of those capabilities across jurisdictions and that at least one city has independently benchmarked useful accuracy against staff review.

The fact that CivCheck tackles document/code compliance — a more complex downstream rules problem than many binary/triage permit-applicability questions — makes it harder to treat cross-city regulatory-rule maintenance itself as a meaningful moat for ProjectPermit.

The remaining defensibility is narrower:

> ProjectPermit's exact upstream `scope + municipality/address -> permit applicability` machine contract, deterministic evidence/version history, fail-safe unknown states and very low-cost developer-native delivery are still differentiated in contract shape, but none has buyer validation.

## Why this is not double-counting LandLogic/Parcella

LandLogic/Parcella already eliminated the remaining **competitive headroom** by showing maintained cross-city property/planning/permit intelligence, white-label embedding and APIs in Ontario.

CivCheck adds a different evidentiary class:

- multi-jurisdiction regulatory/code maintenance as a reusable commercial product;
- real municipal deployments/pilots across different jurisdictions;
- independent measured accuracy against municipal staff review;
- fast/configurable implementation claims backed by a product designed explicitly for many local governments;
- acquisition into an established permitting-software distribution channel.

The score movement is therefore applied only to **defensibility**, not competitive headroom, pain, volume or willingness to pay.

## Future implications

The remaining 1/10 defensibility should fall to 0/10 if CivCheck/Clariti or another delivered platform demonstrates one of the following at credible economics:

1. direct permit-type / permit-necessity identification from project scope before application documents exist;
2. an externally callable API that returns municipality-specific permit-requirement/applicability output;
3. transparent evidence/version/unknown-state handling comparable to ProjectPermit's narrower contract;
4. cross-province Canadian coverage that makes ProjectPermit's seven-city maintained ruleset merely a thinner duplicate.

Conversely, the score should not be rescued merely because CivCheck remains human-in-loop or downstream. Rescue still requires evidence that real software buyers value and will pay for ProjectPermit's narrower upstream contract.
